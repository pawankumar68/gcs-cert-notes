## Security Frameworks

Guidelines used for building plans to help mitigate risks and threats
to data and privacy.

### Purpose of Security Frameworks
- Protecting PII (Personally Identifiable Information)
- Securing financial information
- Identifying security weaknesses
- Managing organizational risks
- Aligning security with business goals

### Components of Security Frameworks
1. Identifying and documenting security goals
2. Setting guidelines to achieve security goals
3. Implementing strong security processes
4. Monitoring and communicating results

---

## CIA Triad

A foundational model that helps organizations consider risk when
setting up systems and security policies.

| Component | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Confidentiality** | Only authorized users can access specific assets or data |
| **Integrity** | Data is correct, authentic and reliable |
| **Availability** | Data is accessible to those who are authorized to access it |

---

## NIST Cybersecurity Framework

A voluntary framework consisting of standards, guidelines and best
practices to manage cybersecurity risk.

### 5 Core Functions

| Function | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Identify** | Identify assets, risks and vulnerabilities |
| **Protect** | Implement safeguards — access control, training |
| **Detect** | Detect threats and incidents through monitoring |
| **Respond** | Respond to incidents — contain and investigate |
| **Recover** | Restore systems to normal state after an incident |

> Memory Tip: **I P D R R** — "I Protect Digital Risky Resources"

## FedRAMP

A US federal government program that standardizes security
assessment, authorization, monitoring and handling of cloud
services and product offerings.

---

## GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

A European Union law that protects the personal data of EU citizens.

Key points:
- Organizations must explain why they are collecting data
- Data breaches must be reported within 72 hours
- Fine: Up to €20 million or 4% of annual revenue — whichever is higher

## Counterattack — US vs International Standpoint

### US Standpoint
Counterattack is **illegal** in the US.
Relevant laws:
- Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (1986)
- Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act (2015)

> In the US, you can only **defend** — never counterattack.

### International Standpoint (ICJ Guidelines)
The International Court of Justice updates its guidelines regularly.
A counterattack **may be allowed** only if ALL conditions are met:

- The counterattack affects only the attacker's party
- It is the only option to communicate with the attacker
- It does not escalate the situation
- Its effects can be reversed
