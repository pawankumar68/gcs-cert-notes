## 1. The Growing Demand for Cybersecurity

- Cyberattacks are increasing in frequency and sophistication, driving demand
  for skilled security professionals across every industry.
- Organizations of all sizes — not just large corporations — need
  cybersecurity talent to protect data and systems.

## 2. Introduction to Cybersecurity

- **Cybersecurity** is the practice of ensuring the **confidentiality,
  integrity, and availability (CIA triad)** of information by protecting
  networks, devices, people, and data from unauthorized access or criminal
  exploitation.
- Cybersecurity protects against both external and internal threats.

## 3. The Role of a Security Analyst

Primary responsibilities of an entry-level security analyst:
1. **Monitor systems** — watch for suspicious activity and anomalies.
2. **Protect information and systems** — implement safeguards.
3. **Search for weaknesses/vulnerabilities** — proactively find gaps before
   attackers do.

## 4. Threat Actors

- A **threat actor** is any person or group who poses a security risk.
- Categories include: advanced persistent threats (APTs), insider threats,
  hacktivists, and script kiddies.

## 5. The Role of Security Teams

Security teams bring value to an organization by:
- **Achieving regulatory compliance**
- **Protecting against external and internal threats**

Beyond that, they also help build customer trust, minimize downtime, and
support business continuity.

## 6. Role of Playbooks in Cybersecurity Investigations

- A **playbook** is a manual detailing the operational actions a security
  team should follow during a specific type of security incident (e.g.
  malware infection, phishing attack).
- Playbooks ensure consistent, repeatable responses and help teams act
  quickly under pressure.

## 7. Key Cybersecurity Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Compliance** | Adhering to internal standards and external laws/regulations related to protecting data. |
| **Security Frameworks** | Guidelines used for building plans to help mitigate risks and threats to data and privacy (e.g. NIST). |
| **Security Controls** | Measures/technologies used to reduce specific security risks (technical, administrative, physical). |
| **Security Posture** | An organization's ability to manage its defense of critical assets and data, and to react to change. |
| **Network Security** | The practice of keeping an organization's network infrastructure secure from unauthorized access. |

## 8. Security Audits

- **Security audits** allow security professionals to review an
  organization's security records, activities, and related documents.

## Key Concepts — Assignment: Spot the Phish

- **Phishing tactics** (social engineering) = psychological tricks: urgency,
  fear, impersonating authority.
- **Technical indicators** = definitive, verifiable red flags: wrong sender
  domain, link text pointing to a different actual URL.
- **Content indicators** = suspicious but less definitive: poor grammar,
  generic greetings, unusual requests (e.g. buying gift cards).
- Always verify the **actual sender domain** and the **actual link
  destination** — these are the most reliable, unforgeable signs of phishing,
  more reliable than tone/grammar-based red flags.
